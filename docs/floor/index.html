Camera stays on your machine. Pose estimation runs in this page via WebAssembly; no video is uploaded anywhere. The page is static. The pose model itself is fetched once from Google's CDN.

Must be served over https:// or localhost — browsers block camera access on file://.

Setup

  1. Point the camera at a floor area, ideally from a height and at an angle, so you can see the whole play space and people's feet.
  2. Press START CAMERA and allow access.
  3. Click the four corners of the play area in this order: top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left. Drag any corner to fine-tune. (Or press Use default floor and drag the four dots into place.)
  4. Check that the grid looks glued to the real floor, then press START GAME.
  5. Walk onto tiles. Your feet are the controller — no clicking.

Calibration is saved in your browser, so you only do it once per camera position. No camera? Press Mock players to watch synthetic walkers drive the same pipeline.

The pipeline

camera frame | v pose estimation MediaPipe PoseLandmarker, numPoses = 4 | v foot landmarks heel + foot-index per foot, ankle as fallback | v homography 4 clicked corners -> unit square | v floor coordinates x,y in [0,1] | v grid cell row A..H, column 1..8 -> "C4" | v player tracking stable IDs, One Euro filter, tile hysteresis | v game logic safe / dangerous tiles, CS questions, scoring | v AR overlay tiles + markers drawn back through the homography

Why each step is there

  • Feet, not bounding boxes. A bounding-box centre sits near the chest and swings when someone leans or raises an arm. The contact point with the floor is the feet, so the stance point is the midpoint of the heel and toe landmarks, with the ankle as a fallback and the hips as a last resort.
  • Homography, not a linear grid. Seen at an angle, a square tile is a trapezoid and far tiles are smaller. A projective transform from your four clicked corners is what keeps the virtual grid attached to the real floor — and it is why the circular markers squash correctly as players walk away from the camera.
  • Jitter control. Landmarks wobble a few pixels per frame even when you stand still. Raw, that makes the detected tile flicker between neighbours — scoring and killing you at random. Two defences: a One Euro filter (heavy smoothing when still, light when moving, so it never feels laggy) and hysteresis on the tile decision, which only commits to a new tile once you are clearly inside it or have stayed a few frames.
  • Identity. Pose models return a list of people per frame with no IDs, and the order can change. IDs come from matching detections to existing tracks by floor distance — globally, not greedily — with a timeout so someone briefly occluded keeps their score.
  • Timed resolution. Rounds are judged when the timer expires, not on contact, so crossing a dangerous tile on your way to a safe one does not punish you.

Code and tests

github.com/dericsen/scratch-ar. The pipeline's logic lives in docs/floor/core/ — geometry, tracking, game — with no dependency on cameras or canvases, which is what makes it testable: node tests/floor_test.js runs 42 tests driving the whole chain with synthetic pose landmarks at known floor positions, including two players scored independently on their own tiles.

MediaPipe is isolated in docs/floor/pose.js behind a two-function interface, with a mock implementation of the same interface for testing and demoing.
